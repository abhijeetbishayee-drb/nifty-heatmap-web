Each point is one security against NIFTY 50: horizontal is relative strength, vertical is the momentum of that relative strength, both centred on 100. The trail is the last few periods, so direction matters as much as position — the usual rotation runs clockwise, Leading → Weakening → Lagging → Improving → Leading.

This is an approximation, not the licensed JdK formula. RS-Ratio and RS-Momentum are proprietary to RRG Research; this uses the published approximation — smoothed relative strength, z-scored onto a 100-centred scale. Quadrants and rotation behave correctly, but the numbers will not tie out against a Bloomberg or Optuma RRG.

Sector points are equal-weighted baskets, not the NSE sectoral indices. Yahoo serves no price history for 10 of the 12 sectoral indices this board maps — only a live quote — so a real-index series exists for Banks and IT alone. Mixing two float-weighted indices with 21 equal-weighted baskets would put non-comparable points on one chart, so every sector is built the same way: constituents rebased to 100 and averaged.

Every symbol is z-scored over an identical window. That matters more than it sounds: the same stock on the same day lands in a different spot if normalised over a different amount of history, so anything without enough history is excluded rather than rescaled.

The 3D view puts volatility on the depth axis. RRG has no natural third axis — RS-Momentum is already the derivative of RS-Ratio, so the two are a matched pair and anything on Z is a different measure bolted on. Volatility is that measure here: trailing realised volatility, annualised, one reading per tail point, so the trail moves in depth too rather than implying volatility held still. It answers what the flat plot cannot — how much risk is behind a given relative strength. Sphere size is driven by volatility directly, not by perspective alone, so bigger = more volatile still holds after you rotate the chart; larger spheres are drawn more translucent so the volatile names in front don't hide the quiet ones behind them. Depth is still harder to read than a flat position, which is why this is a toggle beside the 2D board and not a replacement for it — the quadrant call should be made on the 2D plot.

One caveat the 3D view does not fix: RRG is purely relative, so a name can sit in Leading while falling in absolute terms — beating the benchmark on the way down. Volatility on Z says nothing about that.

RRG is a relative-strength tool, not a standalone buy/sell signal.
